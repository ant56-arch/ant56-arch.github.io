What it is

Can a model beat the market?

Sports Edge is a free, one-person project that tries to answer that question in the open.

It publishes a pick for every game in seven leagues, then keeps score in public so anyone can see how the picks turned out.

  • A model for each league

    Seven leagues, seven separate models. Each one is built for its own sport.

  • Every pick is graded

    When a game goes final, its pick is marked right or wrong. The record never resets, and the misses stay on the board.

  • Vegas is the yardstick

    Wherever Vegas has posted a price, the pick shows our win chance beside theirs, so you can see where we disagree.

The models

How a model works.

  • It rates every team

    Each model learns from several seasons of past games and gives every team a strength rating that moves with each result.

  • It turns ratings into a win chance

    For each game it weighs the two ratings with the factors that matter in that sport, such as home field, starters or injuries.

  • It checks itself each week

    It tests new versions on the newest games and only switches when one clearly predicts better.

The terms

Reading the site.

Pick
The side the model thinks is most likely to win. In soccer it can be a draw.
Win chance
How likely the model thinks the pick is. At 70%, it should win about seven times in ten.
Price
What Vegas pays on the pick. At −150 you bet $150 to win $100. At +130 a $100 bet wins $130.
Vegas %
Vegas's win chance for the same side, shown beside ours.
Streak
The current run. "W10 days" is ten winning days in a row. Football counts weeks.

    Questions

    Asked and answered.

    How often do the picks update?

    Several times a day. The daily sports refresh from morning to early evening as lineups and injuries come in, football refreshes through the week, and results are graded overnight.

    What counts as a right pick?

    The picked side wins the game. In soccer a draw can be the pick, and it is right when the match ends level.

    Why does a model disagree with Vegas?

    A model only knows what is in its data: past results, ratings and, in some leagues, starters or injuries. Vegas's price also reflects news and where the money is going. A gap means the two disagree, not that the model is right.

    Do the models ever change?

    Yes. Each one retests itself about once a week during its season and keeps a log of every retrain on its Model page. The record carries on through every change.
